My Story

I didn't chase
the title.

For most of my early career I was a full stack engineer with deep specialties in infrastructure, cloud, and DevOps culture — someone who built things, broke things intentionally to make them stronger, and cared as much about how software got delivered as how it was written.

What I kept noticing was that my teammates looked to me when decisions needed to be made, when a cross-functional conversation needed to happen, or when something had gone sideways and someone needed to stand in front of it. I wasn't chasing that role. Eventually the people around me made clear that formalizing it was the right step — and I accepted because the team already trusted me, not because I wanted the title.

At Western Governors University, I led platform engineering teams responsible for Tier 0 systems supporting 193,000 students and 8,000 staff across a multi-cloud environment — Azure owned identity and IaC, AWS ran the workloads. My teams lived at the seams between both platforms every day.

That experience shaped a leadership philosophy I now bring to every team I work with — and teach to every professional I coach.

Leadership is a two-way street
Engineers need to trust my direction — and I need to earn their trust in return by treating their input as real information that guides decisions. They are my eyes on the ground.
Results belong to the team
My job is to provide clarity, standards, air cover, and the environment for engineers to do their best work. The outcomes are theirs.
Presence during incidents is non-negotiable
There was never a situation room involving my team that I wasn't present for — not to take over, but to support and remove blockers.
The platform serves the business
The platform serves the business. The team serves the platform. The business exists to serve someone counting on it. My job is to keep that chain visible and intact.
